The Eiffel Tower

  • The Eiffel Tower is a world-famous wrought-iron monument located on the Champ de Mars in Paris, France. It was built for the 1889 World's Fair to celebrate the 100th anniversary of the French Revolution and to showcase French engineering skills. A design contest was held to select the structure, which was won by civil engineer Gustave Eiffel and his company. Construction began in January 1887 and took just over two years, finishing in March 1889. Around 300 steel workers assembled the piece, though many Parisian artists initially hated it, calling the structure a giant metal monster. The tower was only intended to stand for 20 years, but officials saved it because it proved highly useful for science and radio transmission.Today, the tower stands at a total height of 330 meters (1,083 feet), and it held the title of the world's tallest man-made structure for over 40 years. The total weight of the monument is roughly 10,100 tons, with the metal frame alone accounting for 7,300 tons.
  • It is composed of 18,038 distinct iron parts held together by more than 2.5 million rivets. Because it is made of iron, the tower changes size with the seasons, expanding by up to six inches in hot summer weather and shrinking in the winter cold. Strong winds can also cause the top of the tower to sway slightly.Visitors can explore three different public levels using either stairs or elevators. It takes over 600 steps to walk from the ground to the second level, which offers exceptional panoramic views of the city.

THE ROYAL BENGAL TIGER

  • The Bengal tiger is a population of the Panthera tigris tigris subspecies. It ranks among the largest of wild cats. It is distributed from India, southern Nepal, Bangladesh, Bhutan to Southwestern China. Its historical range extended to the Indus Basin until the early 19th century, and it is thought to have been present in the Indian subcontinent since the Late Pleistocene about 12,000 to 16,500 years ago. It is threatened by poaching, habitat loss and habitat fragmentation.
  • As of 2022, the Bengal tiger population was estimated at 3,167–3,682 individuals in India, 316–355 individuals in Nepal, 131 individuals in Bhutan and around 125 individuals in Bangladesh. It is the national animal of India and Bangladesh
